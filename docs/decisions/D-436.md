| D-436 | **TLS handshakes are rate-limited per declared sender: 20 a second, burst 100 (`Limits.HandshakeRate`, `HandshakeBurst`); a connection above it is closed before its handshake and counted in `ConnRejected`. Measured first, on the Ubuntu lab box, loopback only (closes the review L5 line and the 1,000-connection measurement line).** Measured once (`VEDETTE_MEASURE=1 go test -run 'TestMeasureLimits/(1000_idle_TLS|handshake)' ./modules/logs/listen`, through the heavy lock, ECDSA P-256 certificate, client and server in one process so every figure counts both sides): 1,000 idle TLS connections (4 senders x 250) completed their handshakes in 0.32 s wall using 1.06 s CPU and held about 35 MiB of heap plus stacks (about 36 KB each, the clients' share included, 2,000 goroutines more; plain TCP is the same MaxConns 1,000 limit); 1,000 sequential connect-handshake-close from one sender took 1.12 s (892 a second) and 1.42 CPU-ms each for both sides, so roughly 0.5 to 0.7 ms of server CPU per handshake with ECDSA (an RSA box certificate costs more). Conclusion: 1,000 TLS connections fit the box (the MaxConns 1,000 and 10 s handshake limits stay; nothing to change). The cost that matters is churn: before this change one declared sender could ask for several hundred handshakes a second up to MaxConnsIP, which is a core of CPU taken from the Ingest path. 20 a second per sender is far above what a device does (it keeps one connection open and reconnects after a failure or a box restart) and takes at most a few percent of a core; the burst of 100 lets a fleet behind one NAT address, or a device after a box restart, reconnect at once. The bucket is per sender IP, asked after the declared-sender check and before a slot or a handshake is paid for, kept only for declared TLS senders (SetSources drops the rest), so its memory is bounded by the sources. A SetSources close (a source removed, its protocol or pin changed) is no longer counted as a failed handshake (review L3). Not rate-limited: plain TCP (no handshake to pay). | A per-source-address connection rate that is refused before the work of the connection is done is how a proxy protects a TLS front: HAProxy's configuration manual has the `src_conn_rate` sample and the `tcp-request connection` rules for it (opened: https://docs.haproxy.org/3.0/configuration.html; only its keyword index was read through the fetch tool, not the semantics, so the design here rests on our own measurement, not on a copied number). The figures above are ours; the limit is a ceiling for a LAN device, not tuned to the box. | 2026-11-13 |
