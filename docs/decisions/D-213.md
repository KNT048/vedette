| D-213 | **IOC matching of syslog connections for `logs` (WP-24 L4, S-LOG-2, S-LOG-4 syslog half, Emily).** (1) **Which connection**: the LAN device that made it and the public destination it went to, read from the line of a device that has a profile, never from the sender of the log. It is data (D-58): a key=value profile names `local_field` (FortiGate `srcip`), `dest_ip_field` (`dstip`) and `dest_host_fields` (`hostname` of a web-filter line, `qname` of a DNS-filter line); a template profile names a `conn_pattern` with the groups `local` and `dest` (MikroTik firewall `proto TCP (SYN), a:p->b:q`). A kind without a profile, and a line the profile does not settle, has no connection: there is **no generic free-text reader** for destinations (missing data beats a false hit). (2) **Anti-framing guarantee (the rule of D-210, stricter)**: a field counts only when it is written exactly ONCE as a `key=value` site outside quoted text, whatever its values (the same value twice is ambiguous too); a double quote that is not a value's own, text glued to a closing quote, an unclosed quote, more pairs than the scan reads (256), two `->` in a MikroTik line, or two matches of the pattern, make the whole line a line without a connection. Addresses are read with `net/netip` on the text as it is (no trimming of quotes or brackets); a FortiGate line has a connection only when `type` is `traffic` or `utm` (profile `conn_when`; event lines, such as an SSL-VPN login failure, carry text an attacker writes and never have one); a MikroTik line only when it is the first packet of a TCP connection (`proto TCP (SYN)`, and not marked `connection-state:established|related|invalid|untracked`, profile `conn_refuse`): UDP, ICMP and TCP with other flags cannot be told from a reply of a port-forwarded server to a listed scanner, so they have no connection; names are lower-case ASCII, at most 253 bytes, at least two labels of 1 to 63 characters from `a-z0-9-`, not starting or ending with `-`, last label not all digits (an IDN is written `xn--` by the devices). Only a private IPv4 address is a LAN device (`IocHit.src_ip` is IPv4), and not the box, its gateway or a decoy (`Config.NeverCount`, D-210); a destination that is private, loopback, link-local, multicast, unspecified, broadcast, carrier-grade NAT, documentation, benchmarking, reserved, NAT64/6to4 or IPv4-mapped is never looked up. (3) **Lookup**: a `logs.Feeds` port (`Lookup(ctx, kind, value) (Listing{Feeds, Confidence}, found, error)`), adapter in `cmd/vedette` on `feeds.Store.IOCLookup` after `feeds.IOCKey` (so the exact address or the lower-case domain is the key, never a /24); an indicator that no enabled feed lists is not a hit (a switched-off feed is not pulled and not pruned, so its listings are stale; `IOCSighting.Enabled`); confidence is the highest of the listings, kept in [0,1] (the feeds' `alert`/`block` verdicts are computed from the same listings elsewhere and are not stored, so the listing confidence is what logs has). Feeds off: the adapter says `ErrFeedsOff` and `On()` is false, no hit is made, the digest has an empty list (the stored hits are kept) and the page says why (it reads the module state, PRE-3). Lookups are cached for one batch, run before the writer transaction of the batch, are bounded to 5 seconds, and a failure is logged once per run of the same failure and never blocks or drops a line. (4) **Storage and bounds**: `logs_ioc_hits` in logs.db, one row per (device, destination) per 24 hours, at most 10,000 rows (the oldest go), at most 50 new hits per batch (a pair that already has a hit in the last 24 hours is not looked up and does not count toward the 50, so a chatty device cannot hide another device's first hit), kept as long as the module's retention; the device (`asset_id`, `asset_name`) is resolved from the inventory when read (INV-7), so it is not stored. (5) **Events**, published after the batch commits, one pair per new hit: `logs.ioc_hit.detected` (the `IocHit`, for response's `ioc_connection`) and `logs.bad_address.detected` (`source: "logs"`, `target: "ioc:<destination>"` in its own dedupe namespace like `sig:`, title at most 200 characters, plain text with control characters replaced, plus the SIEM fields `id, src_ip, dest, dest_kind, seen_via, asset_id`); at most 100 pairs are announced in a clock hour (a hit past it is stored and in the digest, and the log says so once an hour); announcing uses its own bounded context, so a stopping listener does not lose it. **Known limit**: a name seen in a DNS or web-filter line is attributed to `srcip`, so a domain controller, a DNS forwarder or a proxy becomes "the device" for every client behind it; a response must not act automatically on a name-only hit (`dest_kind` = domain). (6) **SIEM double send** (#344 cross-review): with both `alerts` and `bad_conn` chosen, the `alerts` class leaves out the `notify.incident.created` of kind `bad_address`, because the same connection reaches the forwarder directly as `logs.bad_address.detected`; with `alerts` alone the incident is the only record and is sent. (7) **Read ports**: `IocHit(ctx, id)` and `IocHits(ctx, since, minConf)` (response's `Evidence.IocHit`, adapter in `cmd/vedette`, wired by Kien) and the `known_bad` export source. Nothing leaves the box: the lookup is in the box's own feed tables (PRIVACY.md unchanged). | Why the strict site rule: a hit opens an incident and may drive a response, so a line that an attacker can shape (a user name, a URL, a workstation name carried in a log field) must not be able to frame a device as talking to a listed address; fail2ban CVE-2007-4321 and DenyHosts CVE-2007-4323 were exactly that bug. FortiGate quotes its string fields and writes each key once, so a once-only key=value site is what the format guarantees, and a duplicate is evidence that someone wrote into a field. Why no free-text reader: Wazuh, Graylog and Splunk run their threat-intelligence lookups on the normalised destination field of a decoder/parser (`dstip`, `query`, `dest`), never on a regular expression over the raw message, and Suricata and Zeek feed their intel framework from fields they parsed (`dns.rrname`, `id.resp_h`, `Intel::DOMAIN`). Why the lookup is outside the writer transaction and bounded: the logs writer is shared by the flood path (D-206, INV-12), and Graylog's and Wazuh's lookups are likewise asynchronous with a cache and a failure counter, never a reason to stop the pipeline. Why one hit per pair per 24 hours: Splunk Enterprise Security throttles a threat-match notable by source and object for the same reason (a device that keeps asking for a name is one story); Wazuh `frequency`/`ignore` is the same. Why the incident target is `ioc:<destination>`: notify groups by (kind, source, target), and ten devices that reach the same address are one incident with ten devices.
