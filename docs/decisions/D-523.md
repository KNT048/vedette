| D-523 | **nmap scan shapes tuned to nmap's own guidance (TUNE-1b: tool-tuning plan §1 N2, N3, N7; N4 waits for kien; amends D-84 (7), D-501, D-504).** (1) **Sweep** (`nmapargv.Sweep`): `--privileged -sn -PE -PP -PS21,22,23,80,135,139,443,445,554,3389,8000,8080,9100 -PA80,443 -n --max-rate R`. nmap's `-sn` default for an address outside the box's segment is only ICMP echo, SYN 443, ACK 80 and ICMP timestamp, which a Windows PC with its firewall on drops; the extra SYN ports find Windows (135/139/445/3389), cameras and recorders (554/8000), printers (9100: a bare SYN, answered with RST by the box's kernel, no payload) and admin pages (8080). `SweepProbes` = 17 packets a round for an address behind a router; nmap stops once it answers and may send a silent address another round, so 17 is a floor. On the box's own Ethernet segment nmap sends one ARP request instead and none of these. `--source-port 53` from the same chapter is left out: an evasion trick, not a defender's. `sweepWarning` charges the part of the scope inside the box's own networks (`Config.LocalPrefixes`: the container's interfaces and the appliance's cards) one packet an address and the rest 17, says the routed figure is a floor, and warns only when that does not fit the window. (2) **Normal and Versions**: `--max-retries 3` (nmap's default allows 10 retransmissions, `-T4` 6; a LAN loses few, a lossy Wi-Fi client stops stretching a run, and fewer retries are gentler on a device of unknown kind). **`--osscan-limit` is not used** (plan N3 proposed it; review TUNE-1b round 1 M1): a Windows PC with its firewall on has 135/139/445 open and every other port filtered, not closed, so `--osscan-limit` would skip OS detection on the most common SMB device and lose its OS and "pc" kind; the plan's aim (fewer wrong OS guesses) is already met by `minOSAccuracy` (guesses under 85% were never recorded).
