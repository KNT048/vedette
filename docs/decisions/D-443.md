| D-443 | **The syslog listener keeps its UDP and TCP sockets (and TLS on a box with a TLS configuration) open while the logs module is on, with or without sources; the sources decide only whose lines are kept (S-LOG-1, walk 4; replaces "no source, no socket" of WP-24 L2 and the per-source TLS socket of D-214).** (1) **Why**: walk 4 sent syslog from 192.168.18.145 before any source existed; core had no socket, so the datagrams met a closed port (podman publishes 514/udp+tcp on the host either way: `nc` to 514/tcp connects at the host and is then refused inside) and nothing was counted, so the D-421 hint ("<ip> sends syslog over UDP, but no source has this IP") could not appear, and the page said only "No device is sending logs to the box yet". The same held for a device sending TCP while only UDP sources existed. (2) **What**: `listen.Server.applyLocked` opens UDP and TCP always, TLS when `Config.TLS` is set; `SetSources` no longer opens or closes sockets, it only cuts the connections of a sender that is gone or no longer a source of their protocol (as before). An undeclared sender is counted in the existing 256-entry LRU (address from the socket, protocol, count, last time; never line text) and dropped: UDP datagram discarded, TCP closed right after Accept before any read, TLS closed before any handshake (D-214 (3)), so an open socket costs an undeclared sender nothing more than a TCP or TLS source already allowed. `GET /logs/unknown-senders` (D-421, 10 most recent, operator and up) then names it on the Sources page with **Add as source**. (3) **Unchanged**: module off = job cancelled = every socket closed (SYS-5, `TestNoSocketWhileTheModuleIsOff`); a bind failure is retried every 30 s and logged once per reason; nothing from an undeclared sender is stored (FORBID-5). (4) **The form says it** (walk 4 line 3): the protocol choice of the source form carries one sentence on what the box keeps ("The box listens on UDP and TCP and keeps this device's lines from either." for UDP; TCP and TLS per D-444). | Every leading syslog receiver listens on its port and filters senders after receiving, and says what it dropped: rsyslog `$AllowedSender` discards (and logs) messages of a disallowed sender at an `imudp`/`imtcp` listener that is up; Wazuh `remoted` listens on the `<remote>` port and logs "Message from '<ip>' not allowed" for an address outside `allowed-ips`; Splunk's network inputs listen on their port and `acceptFrom` refuses other hosts. A closed port cannot say anything, which is the walk 4 failure. Not chosen: open the sockets on the first source only (keeps the first device of a fresh box silent, the exact walk case); a "listen" switch on the page (an option nobody needs: turning the module on is the user's yes to receiving syslog).
