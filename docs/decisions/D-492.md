| D-492 | **External exposure names the device that probably answers the open ports, and what is exploited on it (S-EXP-1, walk finding W1-23).** The page listed "SIP 5060, HTTPS 10443" but never tied them to a device, though in a small business the box on the public IP is almost always the gateway or firewall itself (a FortiGate SSL-VPN on 10443 is the CVE-2024-21762 class), whose exploited CVEs Vedette already holds as findings. `GET /exposure/targets` gains `gateway` (null when no IP target has open ports): `{ip, device_id, name, open_exploited}`. The gateway is the LAN default route vedette-host reports (the same read the logs and response wiring use), matched by IP to an inventory device; `open_exploited` is that device's open findings (confirmed or suspected, every finding is a KEV match). Exposure sees a narrow port (`Gateway`, `modules/exposure/ports.go`) wired in `cmd/vedette/exposuregateway.go`; it reads no other module's tables. Wording never claims the port is on the gateway: "answered by your gateway X (ip), or a device it forwards them to" (NAT port-forwards exist). Honest cases: gateway unknown (no host, or the host cannot read it) says Vedette cannot tell which device answers; gateway not in the device list says so; a gateway with no open findings says Vedette knows of none, not that it is safe. Only the box's own IP targets count, not the subdomains of a domain (those often point at a hosting provider). Device names are untrusted labels: control characters removed and cut to 80 characters on the server, rendered as text. Not done here (deferred): the "reachable from the Internet" mark on those findings and sorting them first, which needs the findings module to learn the gateway device; the link to the device's findings already puts them one click away. Sources: Tenable VPR and Rapid7 Active Risk both raise a vulnerability's priority when the asset is internet-facing (https://www.tenable.com/blog/what-is-vulnerability-priority-rating-vpr, https://docs.rapid7.com/insightvm/active-risk/); CISA BOD 22-01 and the KEV catalog guidance put exploited vulnerabilities on internet-facing systems first (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-22-01-reducing-significant-risk-known-exploited-vulnerabilities); Censys and Shodan attack-surface products link an exposed service to the known vulnerabilities of its product (https://censys.com/platform/attack-surface-management/, https://www.shodan.io/). | 2026-11-13 |
