| D-432 | **Logs IOC and storage: five decisions that close the open L1/L4 lines.** (1) **Writer and search at 5 million lines, measured once (2026-11-13, one run of `TestMeasureFiveMillionLines`, `VEDETTE_MEASURE=1`, Ubuntu box, 5,000,000 rows of about 200 bytes over 6 days; the table fills in 44 s):** `Ingest` of 500 lines takes 7.9 ms per batch with the table full (the writer is not slowed by the table size); `GET /logs/events` with no keyword 5-7 ms (the `ts` index, `total` capped at `CountCap`); a common keyword 45 ms (1 h) to 108 ms (24 h, 7 d: 200 lines found at once); a keyword that is rare or matches nothing is a scan of the window: 106 ms (1 h), 2.3 s (24 h), and past the 5 s `SearchTimeout` for 7 d (503 `server_busy`, with the message that says to choose a shorter range or a more specific keyword; at most `MaxSearches` = 2 at once). **Decision: `logs_events` stays in `logs.db` (no own SQLite file)**: the writer cost does not grow with the table and a search runs on the reader pool, so a second file would buy nothing; the bounded scan with its 503 is the designed answer at the byte cap (D-200). Upgrade path if a customer needs full-text search over days: an FTS5 index (`unicode61`), a migration with its own review. (2) **The device of a hit is resolved from the inventory by `src_ip` when it is read** (INV-7): kept. After a DHCP change an old hit shows the address's new holder; storing the device id at write time breaks INV-7 (a renamed or deleted device would show the old name) and the inventory itself keeps the address-to-device history only as far as it knows. Grafana and Elastic security events likewise show the asset resolved at view time with the raw `source.ip` beside it; the hit row always shows `src_ip`, so the person can tell. (3) **The `alert`/`block` verdict of `feeds.Judge` is not used for hits**: logs keeps the highest listing confidence of the enabled feeds and the incident severity follows it (>= 0.7 high). The verdict adds two things, a whitelist and "two independent sources", and the customer whitelist does not exist in the product (`feeds` handlers call `Judge` with an empty `Whitelist{}`), so nothing is lost; a `block` floor would repeat what the confidence of a block-class feed already says. When a whitelist ships, the adapter (`cmd/vedette/logsioc.go`) asks `Judge` and drops a whitelisted name. (4) **No cache across batches for the feed lookup**: the lookup is a point read on the indicator table's primary key and a batch asks each value once; with the 50-hit cap per batch and 5 s budget a chatty device costs one indexed read per batch, far below the cost of the batch itself (8 ms above); a cache would add staleness (a feed switched off must stop matching at once) for no measured gain.
