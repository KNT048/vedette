# Notify — Change Impact Map

## Modifying: `notify.Service.Publish(ctx, Notice)`
- **findings** (vulnerability messages), **honeypot/logs** (through `OpenIncident`, from events), **inventory** (new devices). System messages of the incident kind have no incident row and are sent through `cmd/vedette`: **exposure** (exposure alerts, `exposure.go`), **ai** (budget, activity log; `ai.go`), **response** (`response.go`), **feeds** (the CISA list failing to update: both copies failing two checks in a row or 6 checks, and "up to date again", `feeds.go`): changing `ErrModuleOff` or having `Publish` refuse a message with no `Link` silences those alerts. Changing `Notice` or the `min_severity`/grouping rules changes what users receive; the tests `INV2`, `INV4` must stay green. A finding's notice is judged at `AlertSeverity(cvss, layer)` (layers 1–2 critical, 3–4 at least high): lowering that floor makes a channel's minimum drop findings in layers 1–4 again, silently for the customer except on the History page's "Not sent" list.

## Modifying: `findings.urgency.first_run` (the first-run summary, `firstrun.go`)
- **findings** emits one event per write of an urgency pass (`publishFirstRun`), keyed by its load (`install`, `<source>-<day>`, `catalogue-<day>`). Notify folds the events of one key into one message while a summary of that key waits in the queue (`settle`, 2 to 10 minutes), so changing the key or the id prefix `nt-first-run-<key>-` splits one load into several messages again. The summary's lines are `FindingInfo.Reasons`/`Status`/`Verification` (the finding page's words): a change of those words in findings changes the mail.

## Modifying: the findings list link (`findingsList`, `findingsListOf`)
- **findings** web: `/findings?tab=device&status=<codes>` uses the Findings page's tab id and its status filter codes (`OPEN_FINDING_STATUSES`). Renaming the tab or a code makes the links of grouped alerts and summaries open an unfiltered or wrong list.

## Modifying: `notify.Service.OpenIncident(ctx, IncidentInput)`
- **honeypot**, **logs**, **inventory**, **response** (the Respond button at `/response?incident=<id>`). Changing the merge key = changing the number of incidents the user sees.

## Modifying: `ResolvedSince(ctx, event, data, since)`, `incidentOf`, `mergeKeys` (a repeat after a resolve)
- **ids** (hourly announcement dedupe and the hour's summary), **logs** (`IocDedupe` of a bad connection, `AuthDedupe` of failed logins), **honeypot** (the 10-minute hit window), through `cmd/vedette` `incidentResolvedSince` (false while notify is off). Each producer holds a repeat back only while this says false. `incidentOf` is the one place that turns a producer's event into its `IncidentInput` for both `OpenIncident` (the event handlers) and `ResolvedSince`, and `mergeKeys` is the key order both use: changing the merge rule in one without the other makes a producer hold back a repeat that notify would open as a new incident, or flood notify with repeats it only counts. `TestResolvedSinceSaysWhenARepeatIsANewIncident`, `TestResolvedSinceFollowsTheAttemptMergeRule`, `cmd/vedette` `TestWiringARepeatAfterAResolveOpensANewIncidentAndAlerts`.

## Modifying: `notify.Service.SendReport(ctx, to, ReportMail)` and `IncidentsIn(ctx, from, to, top)`
- **reports** (`cmd/vedette/reports.go`: `reportsMailer` mails the periodic report through the saved email channel: the report's sections as HTML with a text alternative (`reportMailDoc`, `channel.Doc`; `TestWiringReportMailCarriesTheReport`), the digest when the document cannot be read, at most 20 addresses, not in the send history; `reportsDetections.Period` reads the incidents with activity in the period by kind, and the top few by severity with their first device). Changing the fixed errors of `SendReport`, or what counts as activity in a period (`last_seen` after its start, `first_seen` before its end), changes the report.

## Modifying: kind `intrusion`, `IncidentOf`, `KindCounts`
- **ids** (`cmd/vedette/ids.go` `idsIncidents`): links an alert to an incident through `IncidentOf` and the same merge key (`dedupeKey`); changing how the key is computed → the alert loses its `incident`.
- **core** (`cmd/vedette/detection.go`): the Intrusion detection overview counts `KindCounts` in the order of `IncidentKinds` (= the web's `DetectionKind`); adding a kind → add it to both `web/src/features/notify/contract.ts` `IncidentKind` and `detection/contract.ts`.

## Modifying: `notify.Service.EmailReady(ctx) (bool, reason)` and `CodeMailer.SendCode(ctx, to, code)`
- **core** (email 2FA sign-in, `/core/account` `twofa_email.available`). Changing the returned reason makes the message on the sign-in page wrong; it must never fall back to password only (core INV-8).

## Modifying: `CodeMailer.Proven(ctx)`
- **core** (`auth.ProvenMailer`): turning on email 2-step and `/core/account` `twofa_email.available` need the email channel's last test (since the last save) to have succeeded. `TestCodeMailerProvenNeedsASuccessfulTest`. Changing it to look at anything that a failed send can flip on the login path would lock people out: login uses `Available` only.

## Modifying: `CodeMailer.SendStepUpCode(ctx, to, code, summary)`
- **core** (`auth.VerifyTwoStep` email method): without this function, two-step approval by email returns 503 `twofa_email_unavailable`. The message must have no link or anything besides the code; `summary` always goes through `channel.Line`.

## Modifying: `notify.Service.SendApprovalRequest`, the event `notify.approval.decided`
- **response** (approvers, expiry 15–30 minutes, 2 people for high risk). Changing the decision payload = breaking the approval flow; empty `Channels` = send nothing (the caller already used `approval_channels`); `notify` does not know the semantics of the action.

## Interface with `response` (approval through chat channels)
The only caller in every item is `response` (through the adapter in `cmd/vedette`). (1), (2) are written; the types of (3), (4) exist too; the listener and (5) remain.
1. **`VerifyApprovalLink(ctx, token, Binding, keyID) (TokenID, error)`**: checks the MAC by the record's `keyID`, writes nothing, **never creates a key**. Changing it = every approval link that is open breaks (`404 unknown`).
2. **`ApprovalRequest.Channels []channel.Type` (empty = send nothing) and `ApprovalRequest.InAppOnly bool`**: `response` filters channels by `approval_channels`; `InAppOnly` → no button, no link, no code. Ignoring `Channels` = sending a request to a channel the user has turned off for approvals; ignoring `InAppOnly` = breaking INV-16 of `response`.
3. **Gate `Config.Decisions`: `Decide(ctx, Decision{Channel, ExternalID, RequestID, Approve, Code}) (DecisionResult{Status, Message, RequestID}, error)`**: the listener authenticates the platform and then calls it, emitting `notify.approval.decided` when the decision has been recorded. `notify` keeps no list of who may approve beyond the channel configuration; every gate is in `response`. **Telegram**: the `telegram-listen` job in `Service.Jobs()` (no change to `cmd/vedette`: `notifyJobs` already registers every job of `Jobs()`), the table `notify_tg_button` (the nonce hash of the button), `channel.TelegramBot` (`Updates`, `Answer`, `ParseTelegramUpdates`), `channel.Button`/`Message.Buttons`. Renaming/changing the `Status` values of `DecideFromChat` = changing the answers and the event; a `request_id` longer than 50 characters = the Telegram message loses its buttons (only "open Vedette" remains).
4. **`TokenRecorder.RecordReplyCode(ctx, ReplyCode{RequestID, UserID, Address, Approve, CodeHash, ExpiresAt})`**: called before sending a personal Zalo code; hashed per `docs/specs/approval-tokens.md` §7 (a fixed vector in the spec, tests on both sides use it). Changing the hash formula = every open code no longer matches.
5. **Registering `response.decision.made`**: removes the buttons/edits the message in Slack/Telegram. **Telegram (`tgbuttons.go`)**: the handler only marks the `notify_tg_button` row (`state='remove'`) when `final=true`; the `deliver` job (`Flush` → `clearButtons`) removes the keyboard with `channel.TelegramBot.RemoveButtons` (`editMessageReplyMarkup`, an empty keyboard), and also marks requests that have expired (`expires_at`) because the event is best effort; `message_id` is taken from the `sendMessage` answer (`Receipt.MessageID`, `SendRecord.MessageID`, shape-checked, 0 = unknown so it cannot be removed). Changing the payload `{request_id, final}` of `response.decision.made` = buttons are not removed (only the expiry path remains). Dropping "subscribe to `response.approval.requested`": `response` calls `SendApprovalRequest` directly after commit.

## Disabling this module
- No message is sent; Incidents and Settings → Alert channels disappear; the notification panel on a finding disappears.
- `core`: email 2FA reports `module_off`, and email-2FA users are locked out until an administrator resets them.
- `response`: no approval channel. `reports`: no automatic sending. Channel/history data is kept.

## Library (packages `modules/notify` and `modules/notify/channel`)

`Service` (below) calls the functions listed here. Changing a signature = fixing `Service` and the tests.

### `channel` (channel adapters)
- `channel.NewSlack|NewTelegram|NewEmail|NewZalo|NewWebhook|NewZaloPersonal(cfg) (Sender, error)`: boundary checks (INV-8), the error is `*ConfigError{Code, Msg}` (codes like the API mock: `bad_webhook`, `bad_token`, `port_mismatch`, …; plus `blocked_host`, `insecure_url`, `insecure_smtp`; `errors.Is(err, ErrInvalidConfig)`). `Sender{Type, Dest, Send(ctx, Message) (Receipt, error)}`.
- Send errors: `*StatusError{Channel, Code, Reason}` (non-2xx HTTP, or an SMTP code), `*ProviderError{Channel, Code, Reason}` (the provider refuses inside a 2xx answer: Zalo `error ≠ 0`, Telegram `ok:false`; not "HTTP 200"), `ErrInvalidMessage`. No error contains a secret or URL. Secrets use `core/secrets.Value` (SYS-4: masked in every way of printing, fmt, slog, JSON; `Reveal()` used only when building the request; the package adds only the helper `isSet`). Every sender prints as a fixed string (`%v`, `%+v`, `%#v`) because fmt cannot call `Format` on an unexported field (kept as a second layer even though `Value` hides the string behind a pointer).
- An HTTP client supplied by the caller is shallow-copied and always gets a `CheckRedirect` that does not follow redirects; the default client has a 15 s timeout.
- `Message` is plain text; each adapter handles it according to its target. Content from a feed (title, device, what, action, items) and the box's link are kept apart: the box's link stays a real link.
  - **Slack**: escapes `& < >`; the mrkdwn formatting characters `*`, `~`, backtick and `_` (except `_` between two letters/digits, e.g. `snake_case`) in feed content are replaced by identical-looking characters with no formatting (Slack has no other way to escape); the link is `<url|Open in Vedette>`.
  - **Telegram, Zalo OA**: plain text, no `parse_mode`, so no formatting is interpreted. But Telegram still turns URLs, `@mention`s and `/command`s in plain text into links/commands by itself, so in feed content every `@` or `/` not attached after a letter/digit (e.g. `://`, `@user`, `/start`, `(/cmd)`) gets a zero-width joiner inserted. The rest is not neutralised: bare domain names, email addresses, hashtags and phone numbers can still be linked automatically by the app.
  - **Email**: one Subject line folded to ≤ 78 characters (ASCII folds at spaces; otherwise RFC 2047, 4 characters per word); addresses are only `local@domain` (no `%`, no display name, no quotes); body quoted-printable.
  - **Webhook**: JSON.
- `EmailConfig`: 587 ↔ `starttls`, 465 ↔ `ssl`; AUTH is sent only after TLS is complete and the certificate is checked; `none` is only for an internal host and never AUTH (unlike the mock: the mock still requires a username/password for `none`; the adapter does not). One SMTP session reads at most 1 MiB from the server (net/smtp has no limit of its own), counted under TLS. `ParseRecipients("a@x, b@y")` splits the `to` field.
- **Signed webhook (checked by the recipient)**: every request has `X-Vedette-Timestamp: <Unix seconds>` and `X-Vedette-Signature: sha256=<hex>` with `hex = HMAC-SHA256(signing key, timestamp + "." + raw body)`. The recipient compares in constant time and rejects an old timestamp (replay protection). JSON body: `event` (= kind: incident|vuln|approval|test), `id`, `severity`, `title`, `device`, `what`, `action`, `items[]`, `url`. Redirects are not followed.
- **The webhook URL is a secret** (`WebhookConfig.URL` is `secrets.Value`: the path/query often contains a token). `Dest()` and `Receipt.To` are only `scheme://host`. So the send history and the UI do not expose the token.
- **Webhook destination rules** (`NewWebhook` checks when saving, the default client checks again at connect time, on the resolved address, so a DNS name pointing to a forbidden address or DNS rebinding is also blocked):
  - https or http; http only for the internal list (`10/8`, `172.16/12`, `192.168/16`, `127/8`, `localhost`, `.local`, `.lan`, `.internal`, `.localhost`), like feeds INV-2.
  - Always forbidden: link-local (`169.254/16`, `fe80::/10`), unspecified (`0.0.0.0`, `::`), multicast, and the box's own address (`WebhookConfig.BoxAddrs`; `cmd/vedette` fills `Config.BoxAddrs` from the last read of the host's `state/net` cache). **Box address unknown (fail closed)**: `Config.BoxAddrs func() (addrs, known bool)` and `WebhookConfig.BoxAddrsFunc` have the same form; `known=false` (the host has never answered) → `NewWebhook` returns `channel.ErrBoxAddrsUnknown` after every field check (a wrong URL is still its own 400), so saving a webhook is **503 `host_unreachable`** (try again later), the Test button and the send pass record the error "does not know this box's own network addresses yet" and send nothing; the connect-time blocker refuses every address while `known=false`. `Config.BoxAddrs` nil = interface only, treated as known (tests, a box with no vedette-host). The `notifyBoxAddrs` adapter in `cmd/vedette` returns `known=false` when the host has never answered since `core` started, but before that first answer it waits at most 2 s (`firstAnswerWait`), so the first webhook save after start-up is checked against that answer instead of a 503; once known it never waits for the host. An IPv4 embedded in IPv6 is unwrapped and then checked.
  - Loopback (`127/8`, `::1`, `localhost`, `*.localhost`) is forbidden for **both http and https**: the box's own services (the update API, the local AI model) listen there and a webhook redirected into them is SSRF. The only exception is the unexported field `allowLoopback` that tests in the package set for an httptest server.
  - Vedette itself (`ownAddr`, the same rule and the same sentence `channel.OwnText` as feeds, logs, ai): loopback and the process's `net.InterfaceAddrs()` (the core's container; the `channel` package re-reads them every time) are forbidden on **every port**; the machine's addresses (the static `WebhookConfig.BoxAddrs` and `BoxAddrsFunc`, asked again at **every connection** and when saving; `cmd/vedette` fills `Config.BoxAddrs` = `machineAddrs`: the host's `state/net` cache plus the container's gateway) are forbidden only on ports 8443, 8444, 8765, 514, 6514 (`vedettePorts`), so a webhook/SMTP to another program on the machine at its own port is allowed. Changing the port list = changing all four copies (feeds `dial.go`, `channel/webhook.go`, logs `siem_send.go`, ai `provider/client.go`) and the SPA mock. A connect-time error `errOwnDial` → the send history shows `channel.OwnText` (`deliver.go`).
  - Webhook payload (matches the mock): `{event: "incident.created"|"vuln.created"|"approval.requested"|"test", kind: <incident kind>, id, severity, title, devices: [{id?, name}], what, action, items?, url?}`; with no `link` there is no `url`.
  - RFC 1918 private ranges over https are allowed (an internal SIEM is a normal target).
  - A host written in numeric form (`2130706433`, `0x7f.1`, `127.1`) is refused.
  - A caller's client (tests) does not go through the connect-time blocker; it is checked only at configuration time.
- **Zalo OA** (checked against the official documentation 2026-11-12: [send a consultation text message](https://stc-developers.zdn.vn/docs/v2/official-account/tin-nhan/tin-tu-van/gui-tin-tu-van-dang-van-ban/index.html), [conditions for sending](https://stc-developers.zdn.vn/docs/v2/official-account/tin-nhan/tin-tu-van/dieu-kien-gui-tin-tu-van/index.html), [access token](https://stc-developers.zdn.vn/docs/v2/official-account/bat-dau/xac-thuc-va-uy-quyen-cho-ung-dung-new/index.html), [error codes](https://stc-developers.zdn.vn/docs/v2/official-account/phu-luc/ma-loi/index.html)): `POST https://openapi.zalo.me/v3.0/oa/message/cs`, header `access_token`, body `{"recipient":{"user_id"},"message":{"text"}}` (≤ 2000 characters), answer `{"error","message","data"}` with `error` 0 meaning success. ### Personal Zalo (`channel.NewZaloPersonal`, unofficial API)

**Risk (the interface always states it, INV-12).** This protocol was reverse-engineered by the community from Zalo Web; Zalo neither publishes nor guarantees it. Zalo may lock an account that uses it, change the protocol or block it at any time; when that happens the channel stops sending (a `*ProviderError` or `*StatusError`, never silent). Use a **dedicated account** for this, not your main personal account. The protocol knowledge comes from zca-js v2.2.0 (MIT, the library that n8n's community Zalo node uses), rewritten in standard Go, with no code copied; see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

**Configuration.** `ZaloPersonalConfig{UserID, Group, Session, Client}`. `UserID` is the numeric ID of the recipient; `Group=true` when it is a group ID (`fields.group` = `"true"` in `contract.ts`). `Session` is a secret (`secrets.Value`) holding the JSON of a signed-in Zalo Web session, in exactly the form zca-js and the n8n node use: `imei`, `userAgent`, optional `language` (default `vi`) and `cookie` as an array of `{name, value, domain?}`, or `{url, cookies:[…]}`, or a string `a=b; c=d` (or a string containing the JSON of the first two forms). Boundary checks when saving (`bad_session`): at most 32 KiB, at most 64 cookies, cookie name `[A-Za-z0-9_.-]{1,64}`, cookie value 8 to 4096 printable characters and no `;` `,` (a value shorter than 8 cannot be masked out of error text without corrupting the text), printable user agent, imei `[A-Za-z0-9._-]{8,128}`. A cookie without `domain` belongs to `chat.zalo.me` and the hosts below it (the default as upstream), and never goes to `zalo.me`, `id.zalo.me` or elsewhere; a `Set-Cookie` without `Domain` is host-only (exactly that host); `Max-Age` ≤ 0, or an `Expires` in the past when there is no `Max-Age`, deletes the cookie. A cookie of another domain is kept but never sent to a Zalo server. **The adapter does not sign in by itself**: the session is created by QR sign-in (`StartZaloQR` in `channel/zalopersonal_qr.go`, rewritten from zca-js's `loginQR`; `zalologin.go` keeps the sign-in in memory until Save).

**Sending (done, `Send(ctx, Message)`).** Plain text, the same `defang` rule and 2000-character limit as Zalo OA; the box's link stays as is. Every call: HTTP through the supplied `*http.Client` (no redirects, each request has a 15-second limit under `ctx`), reads at most 64 KiB, fail-closed decoding, errors contain no URL or secret (the session, imei, every cookie value, and the session key are masked in what Zalo returns).
- Servers contacted: `wpa.chat.zalo.me` (sign-in, fixed in the code) and the service address `zpw_service_map_v3.chat[0]` (person) or `.group[0]` (group) taken from the sign-in answer. Cookies and messages are sent only to a host name that matches **exactly** `^([a-z0-9-]+\.)+chat\.zalo\.me$` (after lower-casing): no IP address, no `[`…`]` (including IPv6 zones such as `[::1%25.zalo.me]`), no percent-encoding, no trailing dot, no bare `zalo.me` or `id.zalo.me`. The address must also be `https`, with no user/password, port 443 or none, no path, no query; the root URL is rebuilt from the checked host name, not taken verbatim. Each call (`call`) checks the same rule again on the host of the final URL before attaching cookies (checked twice, an error in one place is not enough to leak). If any one condition fails it is refused **before** anything is sent there.
- Encryption (AES-CBC, IV all zeros, PKCS#7 padding): the sign-in key of 32 ASCII characters generated from `zcid` and `zcid_ext`; the session key `zpw_enk` (base64 of a 16/24/32-byte key) for every later call.
- 1. `GET https://wpa.chat.zalo.me/api/login/getLoginInfo` with query `zcid`, `zcid_ext`, `enc_ver=v2`, `params` (base64 AES of `{"computer_name":"Web","imei","language","ts"}`), `type=30`, `client_version=685`, `signkey`, `nretry=0`, `zpw_ver=685`, `zpw_type=30`. `zcid` = upper-case hex of AES(the web client's constant key, `"30,<imei>,<ms>"`); `zcid_ext` = 6 to 12 random hex characters; the key = the first 8 even characters of the upper-case MD5-hex(`zcid_ext`) + the first 12 even characters of `zcid` + the last 12 odd characters of `zcid` reversed; `signkey` = MD5-hex(`"zsecure" + "getlogininfo"` + the values of the parameters in key order, not counting `nretry`/`zpw_*`). Sent with the stored cookies, the stored `User-Agent`, `Origin: https://chat.zalo.me`, `Referer: https://chat.zalo.me/`.
- 2. The answer `{"error_code","error_message","data":"<encrypted base64>"}`; `data` decrypted with the sign-in key gives `{"error_code","error_message","data":{"zpw_enk","zpw_service_map_v3":{"chat":[…],"group":[…]},"zpw_ws":[…],"uid",…}}`. `Set-Cookie` in the answer is kept in memory (only valid cookies of `*.zalo.me`, at most 64) and sent with later requests; the stored session is not rewritten.
- 3. `GET https://wpa.chat.zalo.me/api/login/getServerInfo?imei&type&client_version&computer_name=Web&signkey`. zca-js treats this as part of sign-in (it throws if `data` is missing), but uses the result only for file sending and the listener, not for text messages. Decision: still call it (the web client always does; it is not known whether the server needs it to register the session) but best effort, 3-second limit, ignoring the result and errors, so that one slow call does not hold up an alert message.
- 4. `POST <chat|group>/api/message/sms` (person) or `/api/group/sendmsg` (group), `?nretry=0&zpw_ver=685&zpw_type=30`, body `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` `params=<base64 AES with the session key>` of the JSON `{"message","clientId":<ms>,"ttl":0,"imei","toid"}` (person) or `{"message","clientId","ttl":0,"visibility":0,"grid"}` (group). The answer has the same envelope shape as step 2 but encrypted with the session key; success = both `error_code` values equal 0 and `data.msgId` is a non-zero integer or a non-zero digit string (JSON keys are matched with exactly the same case sensitivity as upstream's `JSON.parse`: `{"error_code":5,"ERROR_CODE":0}` is error 5), otherwise it is a `*ProviderError` (Zalo's `error_code`, 0 if the answer cannot be understood).
- A session is reused for 12 hours (`zaloLoginTTL`). Sign-in is **rationed** (repeated sign-ins can get an account locked): after a sign-in fails or is refused, or a session is refused by Zalo (HTTP 401/403, or a non-zero `error_code` in the outer envelope), every sign-in pauses for 1 minute, doubling for each consecutive failure up to at most 1 hour (the `now` clock can be injected); during the pause `Send` returns `*BackoffError{Channel, Until}` without calling Zalo, and one success resets it to 1 minute. The only exception: the first time a **reused** session is refused with an outer-envelope `error_code`, the adapter signs in **once** and tries **once**; a refusal on a fresh session or on the second try is the final result and starts the pause. 401/403 on a reused session drops the session (no retry) and starts the pause. zca-js defines no "session expired" error code and no code has been checked against a real Zalo, so "outer envelope non-zero" is our guess for "request rejected, message not processed"; an error inside the encrypted envelope (the inner `error_code`, for example a wrong recipient) is an error of the message, with no new sign-in. **Never POST a message twice**: an unreadable 2xx answer or one missing `msgId` may mean the message was delivered, so it is a final result (`*ProviderError`) and is not retried. The sends of one sender queue through a semaphore (a channel of capacity 1); a waiter leaves when `ctx` expires.
- Zalo error codes: only `0` is known to be success; zca-js mentions only `216` (treated as "not found" in `findUser`). Other codes have not been checked; the adapter passes the code through unchanged into `ProviderError.Code`.

**Listening for reply messages (approval codes; `channel/zalopersonal_listen.go`, `zalolisten.go`; EXPERIMENTAL, not tried against a real Zalo).** Done according to the notes below, except: only `zpw_ws[0]` is used (no rotation), no compression is requested, old messages are not retrieved (`cmd 510`), only `cmd 501` is read (zca-js v2.2.0 does not read `551`). `zpw_ws` must be under `*.chat.zalo.me`, with no query: if a real Zalo returns something else the listener does not open (checked on the lab line). Changes: `zaloLogin` also keeps `self` (uid), `ws`, `ping` (read from the server info); `Message.Kind = KindReply` is a plain answer going only through `ZaloPersonalTo`. The `zalo-listen` job uses the channel's remembered sender, so one sign-in serves both sending and listening (`savedSender` keeps exactly one sender per channel even if two jobs build at the same time); a refused handshake does not touch the shared session or the channel's back-off, and the listener waits on its own schedule. A listener and a Zalo Web tab of the same account kick each other out (code 3000): the listener waits 10 minutes before taking over again. Protocol notes from zca-js's listen.ts:
- URL: `zpw_ws[0]` of the sign-in answer (an array; the later elements are used for rotation when it closes with a code in `settings.features.socket.rotate_error_codes`), plus the query `zpw_ver=685`, `zpw_type=30`, `t=<ms>`. Only a host matching the same rule `^([a-z0-9-]+\.)+chat\.zalo\.me$` as above is accepted (it is not yet known whether `zpw_ws` always sits under `chat.zalo.me`: check when building the listener).
- Handshake: a WebSocket upgrade GET with `Origin: https://chat.zalo.me`, the stored `User-Agent`, `Cookie` (the cookies of `https://chat.zalo.me`, including rotated cookies), `Cache-Control: no-cache`, `Accept-Language`, and `Sec-WebSocket-Extensions: permessage-deflate; client_max_window_bits` (zca-js asks for compression; it is unclear whether the server requires it).
- Binary frame. A 4-byte header: byte 0 `version` (uint8), bytes 1 to 2 `cmd` (uint16 little-endian), byte 3 `subCmd` (uint8); the rest is UTF-8 JSON. Outgoing frames have the same layout, with the JSON adding `req_id: "req_<n>"` (except ping). Ping every `settings.features.socket.ping_interval` ms (this number is in the `getServerInfo` answer, so getServerInfo is no longer optional when building the listener): `version=1, cmd=2, subCmd=1, data={"eventId":<ms>}`.
- The first frame from the server: `version=1, cmd=1, subCmd=1`, JSON `{"key":"<base64>"}` is the decryption key of this connection (reset on every connection).
- Direct message: `version=1`, `cmd` 501 or 551, `subCmd=0`; group: `cmd=521`. The body JSON is `{"data":"<string>","encrypt":0..3}` (anything outside 0..3 is an error). `encrypt=0`: `data` is JSON. `encrypt=1`: `data` is base64 of zlib-compressed data. `encrypt=2`: `data` is URI-escaped base64 of `iv(16 bytes) | aad(16 bytes) | ciphertext+tag(16 bytes)` AES-GCM (key = base64-decoded connection key; needs ≥ 48 bytes; in Go `cipher.NewGCMWithNonceSize(16)`), and the plaintext is zlib again. `encrypt=3`: like 2 but the plaintext is JSON, not compressed. The result is JSON in which an integer can exceed 2^53 (zca-js uses json-bigint; in Go use `json.Number` or a string). Size limit: `settings.features.socket.max_msg_size`.
- Packet: `{"data":{"msgs":[…]}}` (501/551) or `{"data":{"groupMsgs":[…]}}` (521). Each `msg`: `msgId`, `cliMsgId`, `msgType`, `uidFrom`, `idTo`, `dName`, `ts`, `content`, `ttl`, `quote`, … A **direct text message from another person** is an element of `msgs` whose `content` is a JSON string (not an object; an object is a file/image/card or the recall `{"deleteMsg"…}`), whose `uidFrom` is other than `"0"` (`"0"` is a message sent by this account itself), and the sender is `uidFrom`. An approval code is valid only when `uidFrom` is exactly the configured approver's Zalo ID and the content matches the code (inferred from how zca-js builds `UserMessage`, not tried against a real Zalo; the `msgType` of ordinary text is `webchat`, inferred from `getClientMessageType`).
- An account keeps only one connection: when a second connection opens the server sends `cmd=3000, subCmd=0` and the client closes with code 3000 (`DuplicateConnection`); code 3003 means kicked (`KickConnection`), 1000 is a deliberate close. Consequence: a Vedette listener and the user's own Zalo Web tab will kick each other out; a dedicated Vedette account avoids this.
- Retrieving old messages after reconnecting: send `cmd=510` (person) or `511` (group), `subCmd=1`, `data={"first":true,"lastId":null,"preIds":[]}`; the server answers with a frame `cmd` 510/511, `subCmd=1`.
- The WebSocket library proposed for both this and Slack Socket Mode: `github.com/coder/websocket` (ISC, v1.8.15 dated 2026-06-15 according to proxy.golang.org, a `context`-based API, can set the handshake headers `Cookie`/`Origin`/`User-Agent`, has `SetReadLimit`, supports permessage-deflate). Not yet run.

### `notify` (rules and tokens)
- `MeetsMin(sev, floor)` (INV-4), `Quiet{Validate, Active, EndsAt}` and `Decide(now, minSev, quiet, sev) (Verdict, releaseAt)` (INV-4/5): `Skip` below the threshold, `Hold` during quiet hours (except critical when `allow_critical`), `Send`. Changing it = changing who receives a message and when; tests INV4, INV5.
- `Notice.Sendable` (a vulnerability message needs ≥ 1 named device; an empty name does not count), `SplitSendable` (INV-2, FORBID-1), `Group(ns, Grouping, floor)`, `GroupHeld(ns, floor)`, `Batch.Message() (channel.Message, error)`, `Batch.DueAt` (INV-3). `floor` is the `min_severity` of **the channel being sent to**: grouping is per channel; `Group` drops non-sendable messages and messages below `floor` before grouping, and `Batch.Message` refuses (`ErrUnsendable`) an empty batch, a batch with a non-sendable/below-threshold message, or a batch not built by `Group`. The grouping key = the product (case-insensitive), the window counted from the first message of the batch.
- **Critical is never grouped.** A `critical` message is always a batch of its own, `DueAt` = its own moment, so INV-6 (≤ 60 minutes) holds for critical even when the grouping window is 240 minutes. During quiet hours critical goes at once when `allow_critical`. `high` and below follow the configured grouping window (15/60/240) and `DueAt` = first message + window: `Service.Flush` sends the batch when `DueAt` arrives.
- `Deliver(ctx, id, sender, msg, now func() time.Time) SendRecord`: always returns a record (INV-6), even when the sender panics (records `ok=false`, `error="internal error"`).
- `MintApprovalToken(key, Binding{RequestID, UserID, Channel, ExpiresAt}, risk, now)`, `ParseApprovalToken`, `VerifyApprovalToken` (GET: changes nothing), `ConsumeApprovalToken(ctx, NonceStore, …)` (POST: burns it once), `ApprovalExpiry`, `ApprovalViaChannelAllowed(channel, risk)`. The format is exactly `docs/specs/approval-tokens.md` §4.1; `Binding` is read from the `response_tokens` record by `token_id`; the key is chosen by the record's `key_id` (the library keeps no keyring). High or unknown risk is refused at signing time on every channel that has a link (`email`, `zalo`): a link is a credential that travels with the person; core must check again when it receives the decision. `TokenID.String()` must never be logged.
